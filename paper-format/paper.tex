\begin{abstract}
We study whether an autonomous, LLM-driven optimization loop can discover
algorithmic improvements to a production consensus library while
preserving every protocol-level correctness invariant. Taking
\texttt{etcd-io/raft}---the most widely deployed Raft implementation---as
our target, we build a layered verification harness combining the
in-tree TLA$^{+}$ specification, deterministic simulation testing under
seeded fault injection, linearizability checking of recorded client
histories, and a differential oracle against the unmodified upstream
library. We then drive an evolutionary search in which a large language
model proposes bounded algorithmic edits and configuration variants;
candidates that fail any harness layer are rejected before any
performance comparison is permitted. We report (i)~the design and
injection points of the harness over Raft's existing
\texttt{Node}/\texttt{Storage} interfaces, (ii)~the set of behavioural
anomalies surfaced during harness bring-up, (iii)~the candidate
variants accepted by the harness and their throughput and tail-latency
characteristics relative to the pinned baseline, and (iv)~an ablation
showing which harness layer caught which class of incorrect candidate.
Our central claim is methodological: in a domain with mechanically
checkable safety properties, the harness---not the model---is the
load-bearing artefact, and a sufficiently sharp harness lets a
general-purpose coding agent search the algorithmic neighbourhood of a
mature systems codebase safely.
\end{abstract}

\subsection*{Research Stage Assessment}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \involvementA{} \textbf{Human-generated}: Humans generated 95\% or more of the research, with AI being of minimal involvement.
    \item \involvementB{} \textbf{Mostly human, assisted by AI}: The research was a collaboration between humans and AI models, but humans produced the majority (>50\%) of the research.
    \item \involvementC{} \textbf{Mostly AI, assisted by human}: The research task was a collaboration between humans and AI models, but AI produced the majority (>50\%) of the research.
    \item \involvementD{} \textbf{AI-generated}: AI performed over 95\% of the research. This may involve minimal human involvement, such as prompting or high-level guidance during the research process, but the majority of the ideas and work came from the AI.
